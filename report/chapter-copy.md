## the-question
This page records what a reader using only public sources could learn about {{services.count}} Bengaluru services before starting a case. Each service was read against six expectations a citizen would ask at a counter: cost, documents, eligibility, time, the office that holds the case, and what comes back after submission. The reading produced {{claims.total}} dated, graded claims from {{sources.distinctUrls}} distinct public URLs. Every figure below is derived from those claims at build time.

## cost
Across {{services.count}} services, {{cost.servicesStated}} publish a fee a citizen pays. {{cost.mentioned}} refer to a fee without giving one. A cell counts as stated only when a claim of grade A to C gives an amount or a fee schedule; a claim that says "fee as prescribed" or that payment follows approval counts as mentioned. The BBMP FAQ on khata transfer states a fee of 2% of the stamp-duty value of the sale deed. The Kaveri marriage FAQ states that payment is requested after the officer's approval and names the accepted payment methods, without an amount. The water connection figure comes from a FAQ describing an older route and is marked stale.

## documents
{{documents.stated}} of {{services.count}} services publish a list of documents. {{documents.mentioned}} refer to documents without listing them, and {{documents.absent}} do not touch the question. Documents is the best-covered expectation in the grid. BESCOM's public FAQ lists the deed, khata, tax receipt, and occupancy certificate classes for a new connection, and the Jaladhare page for a water connection lists the deed, khata certificate, sanction plan, and a photograph of the building. For a birth certificate, no reviewed public page lists what to bring. Where a list exists it is usually a list of document classes rather than a case-specific checklist, and several sources add that the exact documents depend on the proposal.

## eligibility
{{eligibility.stated}} services publish a rule that decides who qualifies or which route applies. {{eligibility.mentioned}} touch eligibility without a usable rule, and {{eligibility.absent}} say nothing. HP Gas's FAQ states what an out-of-town LPG transfer involves, and the BBMP civil-registration manual states that a death reported within 21 days qualifies for a free first extract. For e-Khata transfer, the statute requires notice to the Chief Commissioner, but no reviewed page establishes which properties qualify for automatic mutation and which require the manual route. For marriage registration, no reviewed public source addresses which route applies to a given couple.

## time
{{time.stated}} services publish a timeline. {{time.mentioned}} mention timing without a figure, and {{time.absent}} publish none. BESCOM's Janasnehi page states that a name change is completed within 24 hours, and the building plan site-inspection procedure states inspection within 7 days for head-office sanction and 3 days for zonal sanction. For e-Khata transfer, LPG transfer, property tax, and water connection, no reviewed public source gives a timeline. Where a figure exists it is usually for one step, such as inspection or connection, not for the case as a whole.

## owner
{{owner.stated}} services let a citizen identify the office that holds their case. {{owner.mentioned}} name a statutory authority only, and {{owner.absent}} name nobody. Owner is the least-covered expectation. The Kaveri dashboard lists Sub Registrar Offices by name for marriage registration, and Karnataka's e-JanMa material states that deaths in BBMP areas are registered by Health Officers and Health Inspectors. The BBMP Act names "the Chief Commissioner or authorised officer" for khata and property-tax transfers, and the Karnataka Municipal Corporations Act names "the Commissioner" for trade licences; neither tells a citizen which office to visit. For LPG, new electricity, BESCOM name transfer, and both water services, no reviewed public source names an owner.

## after-submission
{{after-submission.stated}} services expose something a citizen sees after submitting: a status page, a tracker, an acknowledgement, or a downloadable result. {{after-submission.mentioned}} refer to an outcome without showing it, and {{after-submission.absent}} show nothing. The public e-Khata Application Status page accepts an EPID and shows stage, dates, and remarks, and BESCOM's New Connection Tracker accepts a Request ID. Two of the e-Khata status pages observed during the reading returned only "No Data Available". For birth certificate and LPG transfer, no reviewed public source shows what comes back.

## what-this-means
The pattern in the grid is that the public record names an expectation and stops. {{grid.mentioned}} of {{grid.total}} cells are in that state. A fee exists but is "as prescribed"; an authority exists but is "the Commissioner"; documents exist but "depend on the proposal". The record is fullest on what to bring and thinnest on who holds the case and how long it takes. Roadblocks classed as documentation number {{roadblocks.full.documentation}}, against {{roadblocks.full.infrastructure}} classed as infrastructure, and {{roadblocks.stripped.documentation}} of the documentation roadblocks remain after login-related items are removed. This page does not establish why the record stops where it does, or whether a counter would answer what the page does not.

## what-we-do-next
Each cell in the grid is a location where a specific answer is missing. The next phase turns those locations into requests for information that a person who has completed the process, or an office that owns it, can answer with evidence. Answers enter the ledger through the same grading, review, and contradiction rules that produced these figures, and the grid is regenerated. Corrections to any figure on this page are handled the same way.

## method-and-limits
The reader was a set of research passes over public sources: official pages, forms, laws, portals viewed without authentication, and public first-person accounts with personal details removed. No pass logged in, paid, uploaded personal data, or submitted an application, and no private or undocumented interface was used. Each service was audited at least once; {{audits.count}} audit files exist. Claims were graded A to F or Unknown by source strength, dated, and marked as observation or inference. The grid uses a written keyword rule to propose each cell's state and a human review to confirm it; {{grid.reviewed}} of {{grid.total}} cells have been reviewed, and unreviewed cells are marked in the expanded view. Counts are as of the research window shown above and change as the ledgers change. This page is independent research about public documentation, not official guidance, and it does not describe what happens in a live case.
